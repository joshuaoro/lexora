# Interview Guides — Specialists and Parents

**Three short, semi-structured guides for the interviews the proposal's Data
Collection names.** They are guides, not scripts: ask the questions in your own
words, follow what the person says, and skip what does not apply.

| Guide | With | When | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | The three reading specialists | Before development is final (SDLC Phase 1) | The reading-access needs in instrument 06 Part A; the level descriptors; what the reports should show |
| B | Parents or guardians — **key informants, not respondents** | At enrolment, after consent | The intake sheet; design of accessible features |
| C | The three reading specialists | At close-out, after the questionnaires | The qualitative analysis (data guide §5.7); the next iteration (SDLC Phase 6) |

> **To the researchers.** Parents are key informants: the proposal is explicit
> that they "are not themselves respondents in the evaluation", so nothing they
> say is scored or tabulated as a finding. Do not audio-record an interview
> unless the person has agreed to it in writing; notes are enough. Write notes
> **by theme** as you go (the themes are suggested under each guide) — the
> qualitative analysis organizes them that way, and sorting afterwards loses
> what the person meant. Keep notes under codes (`S1`–`S3`, `P1`–`P5`), never
> names. Requires adviser and ethics-committee review, like the rest. Delete
> this box before printing.

## Guide A — Requirements consultation with the reading specialists

*About 30 minutes, one specialist at a time or together.*

**Reading-access needs** — these become the rows of instrument 06, Part A, in
the specialists' own words.

1. When the children you work with read on paper or a screen, what gets in
   their way? (Letters too close? Losing their place? Glare? Speed of a voice?)
2. What do you already do about it — overlays, rulers, larger print, reading
   aloud to them?
3. Which of these would you want a child to be able to change themselves, and
   which should you set for them?

**Content and sequence**

4. How do you use the Marungko sequence with these children? Where do they
   usually get stuck?
5. Which phonological-awareness tasks do you use — blending, segmenting,
   rhyming, first sound, others? In what order?
6. Are there words you would never use with them, and why?

**Levels and assessment** — for Objective 4.

7. How do you decide a child's current decoding level? What would you need to
   see to move them on?
8. What would make you disagree with a level an application assigned?

**Progress information** — for Objective 5.

9. What do you record about a child's reading now? What do you wish you had?
10. Which errors matter most to you when planning the next lesson?

*Themes for notes:* access needs · content and sequence · assessment of level ·
information needed · concerns.

## Guide B — Parents and guardians (key informants)

*About 15 minutes, in the language the family prefers.*

1. What is reading like for your child at the moment? What do they find hard?
2. What language(s) does the family speak at home?
3. Has your child used a tablet or computer before? For what?
4. Is there anything that makes reading easier for them — bigger letters,
   someone reading along, a quiet room?
5. Is there anything about your child we should know to keep them comfortable
   during the sessions?
6. Do you have any concerns about the recordings or the application?

*With the child, if they assent and it suits them:* "If you could choose the
colours and the letters on the screen, what would you pick?" — the proposal's
"children's own preferences regarding the reading interface".

*Themes for notes:* reading challenges · home language · device familiarity ·
what helps · concerns.

---

## Guide C — Close-out interview with the reading specialists

*About 20 minutes, after instruments 03 and 06 are completed, so the interview
explains the ratings rather than shaping them.*

1. Walk me through a session you remember. What worked? What did not?
2. Where did a child get confused or need you to step in?
3. Were there moments the scoring disagreed with what you heard? What was going
   on in those readings?
4. Did the practice lists and levels match your sense of each child? When they
   did not, why do you think?
5. Which part of the progress reports would you actually use in planning? Which
   would you drop?
6. What would you change first?
7. Is there anything you rated on the questionnaire that you would like to
   explain?

*Themes for notes:* usability issues · accessibility issues · technical errors ·
points of confusion · scoring · levels and practice lists · usefulness of
reports · recommendations.
