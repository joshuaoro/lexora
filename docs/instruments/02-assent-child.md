# Child Assent — DRAFT

**For children aged 7–12. Read this aloud to the child. Do not hand it to them
to read.**

> **To the researchers:** this is a draft requiring adviser and ethics-committee
> review, and probably a Cebuano version. See [`README.md`](README.md).
>
> **Why it is read aloud and not handed over:** every child in this study has a
> reading disability. Giving them a page to read would test the exact thing the
> study is trying to help with, and a child who cannot read the form cannot
> consent to what it says. Read it slowly, in the language the child is most
> comfortable in, and stop after each part to check they follow.
>
> Delete this box before printing.

---

## How to run this

1. Sit at the child's level. Make sure the parent has already given consent.
2. Say each part in your own words if that is clearer. It does not have to be
   word for word — it has to be understood.
3. **Pause and ask "May tanong ka?" after each part.**
4. Let them tick the face themselves, or point and you tick it.
5. If the child says no, or seems reluctant, **stop.** They do not take part.
   Tell them that is completely fine — because it is.
6. **Ask again at each session.** A child who agreed in week one may not want to
   continue in week five. That is their right, and nobody should have to justify
   changing their mind.

## Filipino — read this aloud

> Kumusta! Ako si `[pangalan]`.
>
> May ginawa kaming laro sa computer na tinatawag na **LEXORA**. Tumutulong ito
> sa mga bata na magsanay magbasa ng mga salitang Filipino.
>
> Gusto naming malaman kung maganda ba ito at kung nakakatulong. Kaya
> tinatanong ka namin kung gusto mo itong subukan.

**Ano ang gagawin mo:**

> Titingin ka sa isang salita sa screen. Pipindutin mo ang mikropono at babasahin
> mo ito nang malakas. Makikinig ang computer at sasabihin niya kung tama ang
> pagbasa mo.
>
> May mga laro rin tungkol sa tugma at sa unang tunog ng salita.
>
> Minsan, may lalabas na **mga salitang imbento** — hindi totoong salita. Parang
> "bimo" o "kelo". Hindi mo iyon dapat alam. Sinusubukan lang namin kung kaya
> mong baybayin ang mga titik. **Walang mali doon.**

**Mga dapat mong malaman:**

> Ire-record ang boses mo habang nagbabasa ka, para mapakinggan ito ng iyong
> teacher at malaman kung tama ang computer. **Ikaw at ang teacher mo lang ang
> makakarinig nito.** Buburahin namin ito pagkatapos.
>
> **Hindi ito test. Walang grado. Hindi ka bagsak.**
>
> Kung mapagod ka o ayaw mo na, **sabihin mo lang at titigil tayo agad.** Kahit
> anong oras. Hindi ka mapapagalitan.
>
> Kung ayaw mo talagang sumali, **okay lang iyon.** Wala kang problema at
> tuloy pa rin ang klase mo dito.

**Tanungin ang bata:**

> May tanong ka ba?
>
> Gusto mo bang subukan?

## English — for the record

> Hello! I'm `[name]`.
>
> We made a computer game called **LEXORA**. It helps children practise reading
> Filipino words. We want to know if it is any good and if it helps — so we are
> asking whether you would like to try it.

**What you would do:** look at a word on the screen, press the microphone, and
read it out loud. The computer listens and tells you if you read it right. There
are also games about rhyming and first sounds. Sometimes made-up words appear —
like "bimo" or "kelo". You are not supposed to know those. We just want to see if
you can sound out the letters. **You cannot get those wrong.**

**Things you should know:** your voice is recorded while you read, so your teacher
can listen and check whether the computer was right. Only you and your teacher
hear it, and we delete it afterwards. **This is not a test. There are no grades.
You cannot fail.** If you get tired or do not want to carry on, just say so and we
stop straight away — any time, and nobody will be cross. If you do not want to
join at all, that is completely fine and your classes here carry on as normal.

**Ask:** Do you have any questions? Would you like to try?

☐ 😊 Yes, I want to    ☐ ☹️ No, I don't want to

## Notes for whoever administers this

**Watch for agreement that isn't assent.** A child who has spent weeks with you
will often say yes because you asked. Signs to take seriously: looking to the
adult before answering, a long pause then a quiet yes, or saying yes while
shaking their head. If you are unsure, say *"Pwede ka rin mag-hindi ha"* and ask
again.

**Withdrawal during a session** is just as valid as refusing at the start. If a
child asks to stop, stop — do not finish the run first. Their words already read
are saved, and the app is built to handle a session left partway.

**Record refusals and withdrawals in the field log**, with the date. A study that
reports five participants and cannot say what happened to a sixth has a hole in
it.
